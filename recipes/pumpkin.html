Pumpkin Pie

Description

Too many pumpkin pie recipes result in a grainy custard in a soggy crust. For our pumpkin pie recipe, we avoided this outcome by drying out the pumpkin puree (and adding roasted yams for complex flavor) on the stove top before whisking in dairy and eggs. The hot filling let the creamy custard firm up quickly in the oven, preventing it from soaking into the crust.

Before you begin

If candied yams are unavailable, regular canned yams can be substituted. The best way to judge doneness is with an instant-read thermometer. The center 2 inches of the pie should look firm but jiggle slightly. The pie finishes cooking with residual heat; to ensure that the filling sets, cool it at room temperature and not in the refrigerator. To ensure accurate cooking times and a crisp crust, the filling should be added to the pre-baked crust when both the crust and filling are warm. Serve at room temperature with whipped cream. Vodka is essential to the texture of the crust and imparts no flavor; do not substitute extra water. The alcohol is key to our recipe; if you don't have vodka on hand, you can use another 80-proof liquor.

Make Ahead: You can make this pie up to 24 hours in advance.

Ingredients

    Crust

  • 177 grams (1¼ cups) all-purpose flour, divided
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • ½ teaspoon table salt
  • 86 grams (6 tablespoons) unsalted butter, cut into ¼-inch slices and chilled
  • 44 grams (¼ cup) vegetable shortening, cut into 2 pieces and chilled
  • 2 tablespoons vodka, chilled
  • 2 tablespoons ice water

    • Filling

  • 231 grams (1 cup) heavy cream
  • 245 grams (1 cup) whole milk
  • 3 large eggs plus 2 large yolks
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 (15-ounce) can pumpkin puree
  • 1 cup drained candied yams from 15-ounce can
  • 149 grams (¾ cup) sugar
  • 75 grams (¼ cup) maple syrup
  • 2 teaspoons grated fresh ginger
  • ½ teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • ¼ teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1 teaspoon table salt

Steps

    For the Crust

  1. Process 106 grams (¾ cup) all-purpose flour, 1 tablespoon sugar, and ½ teaspoon table salt in food processor until combined, about two 1-second pulses. Add 86 grams (6 tablespoons) chilled unsalted butter, cut into ¼-inch slices, and 44 grams (¼ cup) chilled vegetable shortening, cut into 2 pieces, and process until homogenous dough just starts to collect in uneven clumps, about 10 seconds; dough will resemble cottage cheese curds with some very small pieces of butter remaining, but there should be no uncoated flour.
  2. Scrape bowl with silicone spatula and redistribute dough evenly around processor blade. Add remaining 71 grams (½ cup) all-purpose flour and pulse until mixture is evenly distributed around bowl and mass of dough has been broken up, 4 to 6 quick pulses. Empty mixture into medium bowl.
  3. Sprinkle 2 tablespoons chilled vodka and 2 tablespoons ice water over mixture. With silicone spatula, use folding motion to mix, pressing down on dough until dough is slightly tacky and sticks together. Flatten dough into 4-inch disk. Wrap in plastic and refrigerate at least 45 minutes or up to 2 days.
  4. Remove dough from refrigerator and roll out on generously floured (up to ¼ cup) work surface to 12-inch circle about ⅛ inch thick.
  5. Roll dough loosely around rolling pin and unroll it onto 9-inch pie plate, leaving at least 1-inch overhang around edge. Ease dough into plate by gently lifting edge of dough with your hand while pressing into plate bottom with your other hand. Refrigerate 15 minutes.
  6. Adjust oven rack to lowest position, place rimmed baking sheet on rack, and heat oven to 400 degrees. Trim overhang to ½ inch beyond lip of pie plate. Fold overhang under itself; folded edge should be flush with edge of pie plate. Using thumb and forefinger, flute edge of dough. Refrigerate dough-lined plate until firm, about 15 minutes.
  7. Line chilled pie shell with aluminum foil, covering edges to prevent burning, and fill with pie weights. Bake on rimmed baking sheet for 15 minutes.
  8. Remove foil and weights, rotate plate, and continue to bake until golden brown and crisp, 10 to 15 minutes longer. Remove pie plate and baking sheet from oven.

    9. For the Filling

  9. While pie shell is baking, whisk 231 grams (1 cup) heavy cream, 245 grams (1 cup) whole milk, 3 large eggs plus 2 large yolks, and 1 teaspoon vanilla extract together in medium bowl.
  10. Combine 1 can pumpkin puree, 1 cup drained candied yams, 149 grams (¾ cup) sugar, 75 grams (¼ cup) maple syrup, 2 teaspoons grated fresh ginger, ½ teaspoon ground cinnamon, ¼ teaspoon ground nutmeg, and 1 teaspoon salt in large heavy-bottomed saucepan; bring to sputtering simmer over medium heat, 5 to 7 minutes. Continue to simmer pumpkin mixture, stirring constantly and mashing yams against sides of pot, until thick and shiny, 10 to 15 minutes.
  11. Remove pan from heat and whisk in cream mixture until fully incorporated. Strain mixture through fine-mesh strainer set over medium bowl, using back of ladle or spatula to press solids through strainer. Re-whisk mixture and transfer to warm pre-baked pie shell. Return pie plate with baking sheet to oven and bake pie for 10 minutes.
  12. Reduce heat to 300 degrees and continue baking until edges of pie are set (instant-read thermometer inserted in center registers 175 degrees), 20 to 35 minutes longer.
  13. Transfer pie to wire rack and cool to room temperature, 2 to 3 hours. Cut into wedges and serve with whipped cream.
  14. Enjoy!
